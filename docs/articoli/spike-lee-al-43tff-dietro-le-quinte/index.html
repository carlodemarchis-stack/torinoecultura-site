Spike Lee al 43TFF: Dietro le Quinte di una Conferenza Stampa Memorabile

Alla domanda ma come è Spike Lee dal vivo, l'unica risposta di alto livello intellettuale che mi è venuta fuori è: "una sagoma"

Chi mi segue sa che una delle cose che mi appassiona di più del cinema, così come del mondo dei media e dello sport in cui ho sempre lavorato, è andare behind the scenes. Dopo anni passati dietro le quinte nel settore media e sport, quello che mi coinvolge davvero sono le conferenze stampa con registi e attori: quei momenti in cui l’arte incontra la persona, dove le tecniche narrative si intrecciano con le esperienze di vita, dove emergono aneddoti che spesso valgono quanto il film stesso.

La conferenza stampa di Spike Lee al 43° Torino Film Festival per la presentazione di “Highest 2 Lowest” è stata esattamente questo: un viaggio dietro le quinte della mente di uno dei registi più influenti del nostro tempo, che ha saputo trasformare una semplice conferenza in uno spettacolo a sé stante.

Ho deciso di lasciare le sue citazioni tutte in inglese letterale per catturare al massimo il suo pensiero. Al fondo troverete la versione tradotta capitolo per capitolo.

L’Apertura: La Questione Sinner

Lee ha aperto con un momento tanto inaspettato quanto memorabile, sentendo il bisogno di chiarire una polemica nata dal suo apparente sostegno al tennista spagnolo Carlos Alcaraz rispetto al campione italiano Jannik Sinner.

“I just want to clear up something. I have nothing against Sinner. He is a great, great, great tennis player,” ha dichiarato con enfasi. La sala è esplosa in risate mentre continuava: “And because some people so much enthusiasm for Carlos, they felt that that was being disrespectful to Sinner. That is not the case at all.”

La passione di Lee per lo sport, in particolare per i New York Knicks, ha colorato la sua spiegazione: “I love sports, especially basketball, the New York Knicks. So coming here today... when I knew I was coming here, I wanted to say, because I was getting text messages all type of stuff immediately.” Si è fermato, chiaramente trattenendosi dall’usare un linguaggio più forte riguardo alle critiche ricevute.

Il desiderio genuino del regista di fare pace era evidente: “And I hope to meet Sinner one day in person, shake his hand, and let him know I have nothing but love for him. You know? The two, the two, they’re the two best male tennis players in the world. So it’s all love. I have nothing against him.” Ha poi detto direttamente ai giornalisti: “So write that down.”

È emerso il collezionista che è in lui: “And then if I see him, I get to meet him at the Wimbledon or the French Open, or US Open, I’d like to meet him in person. I’m a big collector. So I’d love for him to sign for me a match worn tennis racket. Have a match played tennis from Arthur Ashe, from Serena, from Coco. I’d like to add that, or add Sinner to my collection.”

Il momento si è concluso con la caratteristica schiettezza di Lee: “Please, as journalists, get the word out to Mr. Sinner that nothing but love. L-O-V-E, okay?”

Filosofia di Carriera e Prime Lotte

Quando gli è stato chiesto di aver fatto qualcosa di “pazzo” per costruire la sua straordinaria carriera, la risposta di Lee ha rivelato vulnerabilità e determinazione insieme. “When I told people, friends and relatives, that I wanted to be a filmmaker, they said I was crazy. And I knew I wasn’t crazy. I knew I had found something I wanted to do for the rest of my life.”

Lee ha sottolineato con orgoglio le sue credenziali accademiche: “For those who don’t know, I’m a tenured professor of film at New York University Graduate Film School - the best film school in the world, New York University Graduate Film School. And I’ve been teaching there 30 years. I’m tenured, that means they can’t fire me.” La menzione del compagno di classe Ang Lee e del direttore della fotografia Ernest Dickerson ha sottolineato i rapporti collaborativi formati durante quegli anni formativi.

La sua filosofia su lavoro e passione ha risuonato profondamente: “And I say my blessings every day ‘cause I’m doing what I love. And if you could make a living doing what you love, you are blessed. Because the majority of people on this God’s Earth get up every morning and go to a job that they hate.”

Lee ha dipinto un quadro crudo della realtà di molte persone: “And then they’re not gonna let, they’re gonna want to put clothes on their children’s back, a roof over their head, food on the table, so they’ll provide for their family. Or they get up in the morning and they got to go to work. That does not make them happy. So I’m blessed.”

Riflettendo sulle sfide del suo percorso, Lee ha ammesso: “When you’re a filmmaker, even the great, great filmmakers have projects that got delayed or never got made. So why should I be special? There’s trials and tribulations to being a filmmaker.” La sua strategia di sopravvivenza era semplice: “My thing is keep going, keep going, keep going.”

L’entità del suo primo successo con “She’s Gotta Have It” lo stupisce ancora: “My first film, She’s Gotta Have It, which was titled Lola Dawn here in Italy, next year is gonna be the 40th anniversary. 40 years. I didn’t even know it until somebody pointed out. That film almost killed me. We shot it 12 days, two six-day weeks. The budget was $175,000, and we made eight and a half million.”

Ha descritto il finanziamento precario: “And the 175... that $175,000 was put together piecemeal. First we shot it. Then we had to get the film out of the lab. Then I had to have money raised to edit the film, ‘cause I felt that... I was confident enough, that if I could just get a rough cut, I could screen it for potential investors.”

La lotta tra scuola di cinema e successo era reale: “But another look at that, I didn’t just go from film school to She’s Gotta Have It. There was a three or four-year gap where I was struggling. I mean, I was struggling. I was broke. But I just had a dream, and I was gonna, short of committing a crime, I was gonna do what I had to do to get this film done.”

Collezione d’Arte e Design di Produzione di “Highest 2 Lowest”

La discussione sulle opere d’arte in “Highest 2 Lowest” ha rivelato l’approccio sofisticato di Lee alla narrazione visiva e la sua pragmaticità cinematografica. Quando è stato interrogato sui pezzi prominenti di Basquiat, Kehinde Wiley e altri artisti contemporanei visibili nel attico di Denzel Washington, Lee ha spiegato la strategia narrativa.

“Well, I’m glad that you noticed the art in the penthouse, where Denzel’s character lives. So it was very simple. The audience knows from the opening credits... he has money.” Lee ha illustrato la logica visiva: “’Cause we had that drone shot that goes over the East River, over by this building, it’s called the DUMBO Olympia. That’s the area of Brooklyn, neighborhood called DUMBO. So right away, he’s on a phone at his penthouse. Then you go inside his house, the penthouse, and the art on the wall. So right away, the audience knows this Denzel’s character has money.”

Tuttavia, l’esperienza pratica di Lee nel cinema ha informato le sue scelte: “And a lot of that art in the penthouse was my and my wife’s art collection. But it wasn’t the real art. It was copies of it. Film crews are notorious. So those pieces cost a lot of money, so I really did not want them damaged.” Questa rivelazione ha suscitato risate consapevoli tra i professionisti del settore presenti.

La Mia Domanda: Il Processo di Scrittura delle Sceneggiature

La mia domanda sulle sceneggiature ha fatto emergere un dettaglio affascinante sul processo creativo profondamente personale di Lee. Quando ho menzionato di aver comprato i suoi libri di sceneggiature a New York negli anni ‘90 e ho chiesto del suo attaccamento a quelle sceneggiature e se le conserva come parte della sua abitudine al collezionismo, la risposta di Lee ha rivelato il suo rapporto unico con la parola scritta.

“Oh, yes. Recently, I’m on Instagram. And if you want to join, it’s Official Spike Lee. And recently, I posted the first page of my scripts, several scripts. I don’t type.”

Lee ha poi spiegato il suo metodo distintivo: “So I write pen, am I left-handed? I’m left-handed, and I write. And then once the script is finished, then I send out to somebody to type it up. But my brain is bad. I failed my typing class in school.”

Ha sottolineato la connessione fisica cruciale nel suo processo creativo: “So for me, just for me, I work not like anybody else. Typing, I need, it goes brain, hand, pen. That’s what I work. No typing. My scripts are written, hand written.”

Questo approccio analogico nell’era digitale riflette l’impegno di Lee nel mantenere una creatività tattile: “And that’s why, go to Instagram you’ll see several of those [first pages].” La sua volontà di condividere questi intimi artefatti creativi con i fan dimostra la sua comprensione di come il processo possa essere tanto avvincente quanto il prodotto.

Filosofia della Reinterpretazione vs Remake

L’approccio di Lee all’adattamento di “High and Low” di Kurosawa ha rivelato la sua comprensione sofisticata dell’influenza artistica e dell’interpretazione. “Here’s the thing, very good question. I do not do remakes.”

Ha elaborato sulle sue fondamenta da scuola di cinema: “In film school, one of the great things about film school, especially at NYU, was that I was introduced to world cinema. De Sica, Fellini, Kurosawa.” L’influenza è stata immediata e profonda: “And the premise for Rashomon, I used for She’s Gotta Have It. I mean, there’s even a term called Rashomon effect. Meaning, when you have 10 people, 10 people have a different opinion. And so I used that for Lola Dawn, where the main character, Lola Dawn, had three boyfriends at the same time.”

Incontrare Kurosawa rimane un momento culminante della carriera: “I got to meet the master filmmaker Kurosawa. And in my editing room is a beautiful portrait of him, and he signed it to me. He did his signatures in white ink with a paintbrush.” Lee ha descritto la presenza spirituale durante il montaggio: “And while we were editing the film, we placed the autographed portrait very strategically, so he was looking over my shoulder while we were editing the film. And I felt his presence. So anytime there was something my editor Barry, my longtime editor, Barry Brown, and I would have, we don’t know, I would turn my shoulder. What do you think, Kurosawa? What do you think? Mr. Kurosawa, what do you think? And he would let me know. Yea or nay.”

La sua analogia jazz ha catturato perfettamente il suo approccio: “This is a reinterpretation. Not a remake. I’ll give an example. Many of you are cinephiles, so you’ve seen The Sound of Music, and you saw Julie Andrews singing My Favorite Things. Classic. Now, let me give you an example of reinterpretation. John Coltrane. Listen, look. No hate on Julie Andrews. There’s two great things. Rodgers and Hammerstein, Sound of Music, My Favorite Things. John Coltrane, a jazz interpretation of a great song. So that was my thing. On this film, I was not doing a remake. I was doing a jazz interpretation of Kurosawa’s High and Low.”

Anche il cambiamento del titolo rifletteva questa filosofia: “In fact we even changed, kind of twisted the title. It’s not High and Low, it’s Highest 2 Lowest. And it’s the number two.”

L’Incontro con il Papa: Un Cinefilo Inaspettato

L’incontro di Lee con Papa Leo ha fornito uno dei segmenti più divertenti della conferenza, mostrando la sua capacità di trovare connessioni inaspettate oltre i confini culturali.

“Here’s the thing. I knew nothing about what happened. I got an email from his office. I thought it was fake. Another word in English for fake is fugazi.” Il suo scetticismo iniziale era comprensibile: “And then as I wrote back, I emailed back, ‘Is this for real? Is this legit?’ They said, ‘Yes,’ and it was going to be on cinema. So right away, I was floating. I get an audience with the Pope.”

Le ricerche di Lee hanno rivelato connessioni sorprendenti: “Now, the Pope is from Chicago. And something that you might not know, the Pope, his people, Black. His people... Yes. Do your research. He comes, his family comes from New Orleans. Creole. A lot of intermarriage. So, the Black Pope’s been claimed. We call him our brother.”

La connessione con i New York Knicks ha sigillato l’accordo: “So anyway, once I knew I was going to get an audience with the Pope, I thought automatically, the Pope went to college at Villanova. Villanova University. And the New York Knicks had three players from Villanova. And so, I said, ‘I got to do this, right? If I get the chance, I need to give a New York Knick jersey as a gift.’ So it’s 14 Pope. Number 14, and on the back it said, ‘Pope Leo.’”

Lee ha descritto il momento della connessione: “So when the Pope came out and sat down, I was in the first row. I took the jersey out of the bag and just held it up before we got to... He looked at me before his speech on cinema. And he laughed, he smiled. So that’s where the connection was made. And so when he smiled back at me holding up the jersey, I knew I was safe in presenting it.”

È emerso il collezionista pragmatico che è in Lee: “And I’m waiting to get it back. I autographed for him the one he held up. But I gave him another one to sign for me.” Quando gli è stato chiesto dell’advocacy del Papa per l’esperienza cinema dal vivo, Lee ha risposto: “Yes, I believe him. I mean, he was just... The Pope was telling the truth as he saw it as far as how cinema could be a great impact for good on this God’s Earth.”

L’impatto globale lo ha sorpreso: “So, people never knew who I was, know who I am now, because of this picture of the Pope holding up the jersey I gave to him as a gift. That went around the world.”

Progresso di Hollywood e Cinema Contemporaneo

Quando gli è stato chiesto del tokenismo e della diversità a Hollywood, Lee ha offerto una prospettiva sfumata basata su quasi quattro decenni nell’industria: “And Hollywood has improved because more people come from behind the camera and are writing behind and in front of the camera. These are films, TV, and theater. I mean this is way better now than when I started back in 1986 which was 39 years ago.”

Il suo entusiasmo per “Sinners” di Ryan Coogler era contagioso: “And one of my favorite films this year was Sinners by Ryan Coogler. So if all, everybody here, if you’ve not seen this, please... We all know he did the Black Panther films, but this is something that might be so much [more].”

Lee ha condiviso la storia fortuita di aver visto il film: “Funny story, I was in LA and the phone rang and it was Ryan. And he said, ‘Are you in LA?’ I said, ‘Yeah man.’ I mean there was zero chance I would be in LA that day he called me, but I was there. And he said, ‘You’re here in New York, you’re here in LA.’ I said, ‘Yes.’ He said, ‘I’m getting ready to do the final version of the IMAX, so can you get here in an hour?’ I said, ‘Where you at?’ I got the address, I’ll be there in an hour.”

Il suo verdetto era inequivocabile: “And that is the best [film] out of theater this year. And then there’s a sequence, you see where he does a sequence where he does this whole music thing, different genres of music. I was jumping out of my seat. I mean it was like, it was amazing. So this is my favorite film this year, Sinners, Ryan Coogler.”

Commento Politico ed Eventi Attuali

Quando la conversazione è virata sulla vittoria elettorale di Trump, Lee ha inquadrato attentamente la sua prospettiva: “Everybody please listen. I’m only speaking for Spike Lee. I’m not speaking for every single Black person in the world, let alone the United States of America or the People’s Republic of New York. It’s my opinion.”

Il suo smarrimento per gli sviluppi politici recenti era evidente: “So this meeting they had, to me, I just, I’m trying to understand it because the things that, because the United States said about them, calling them communists, New York City was going to go down the drain.”

Lee ha fatto riferimento a minacce specifiche: “I mean the President said he was going to cut money off, that New York City would not receive any more federal funds. And so with things that were said, they have this quick turnaround. So we shall see what we shall see.”

Rapporto Cinematografico con New York e Influenza Familiare

Quando gli è stato chiesto del suo rapporto in evoluzione con New York da una prospettiva cinematografica, Lee inizialmente si è irrigidito per quella che sembrava una domanda vaga: “My relation with New York? Give me another question. I lived there. What kind of question is that?” Dopo un chiarimento, ha fornito un ricco contesto autobiografico.

“I was not born in New York. I was born in Atlanta, Georgia. And my family moved north when I was three years old. So I’m a New Yorker. I don’t remember not ever living in New York City except when I went back to Atlanta for college. I went to Morehouse College, Atlanta, Georgia.”

L’influenza di sua madre è stata fondamentale: “I mean, my mother was a cinephile and my father hated movies. So I’m the oldest of five and I was my mother’s movie date, and we went to movies.”

Lee ha collegato il suo lavoro attuale alla storia del cinema: “In fact, in this new film Highest 2 Lowest, I’m giving you a French word. That stuff on the subway in Highest 2 Lowest, that’s The French Connection. That’s Billy Friedkin. That, the chase, where the late great Gene Hackman starts as the character of Popeye the Dope, which is a real character, he was a real detective. That chase began at the subway stop by high school. So when I was in high school, I was in class when they filmed that. We didn’t know it ‘til the film came out a year later and we saw the sign.”

La sua esposizione cinematografica precoce era inconscia ma formativa: “So, I was a cinephile even before I knew what the word was. I was my mother’s... My mother was a cinephile. And I was her movie date. So looking back, that’s where the seeds were planted for... I went to the movies going to the movies to have fun. I never thought... I didn’t know who the director was, what they did. I didn’t read the credits. I just went to the movies. So those seeds were planted very early.”

L’influenza musicale della famiglia era ugualmente importante: “And that’s why I’ve come to really learn parents, what parents can do, and what they don’t do. And I’m just grateful my parents, my father agreed to jazz. My father did the scores. He did the score for my films. He did the score for She’s Gotta Have It, School Daze, Do the Right Thing, and both of them movies. So music was always played in the house.”

Lee ha concluso con profonda gratitudine: “So I got introduced to film and music at an early age, not knowing somewhere down the line in my life they would come back and be my life.”

Contesto del Festival e Chiusura

La conferenza stampa si è conclusa con l’umorismo caratteristico di Lee sul viaggio e sull’investimento: “Look, I flew from, I came a long way. Somebody must have a question about the film. All right. Who did you like the film?” Quando il moderatore si è preparato a fare l’ultima domanda, Lee ha scherzato: “Can we get a new moderator, please?”

Riflessioni Finali

La conferenza stampa ha catturato la personalità sfaccettata di Lee: l’artista serio, il fanatico dello sport, l’ospite internazionale garbato, l’educatore appassionato e il regista pragmatico che comprende sia il business che l’arte del cinema. La sua volontà di affrontare le controversie a viso aperto, condividere i processi creativi personali e connettersi oltre i confini culturali ha dimostrato perché rimane una delle figure più avvincenti del cinema a quasi 40 anni dall’inizio della sua carriera.

Per chi come me ama andare dietro le quinte, questa conferenza è stata un regalo: non solo abbiamo avuto accesso al film “Highest 2 Lowest”, ma anche alla mente di un maestro narratore che continua a reinterpretare e reinventare narrazioni classiche per il pubblico contemporaneo. È questo tipo di incontro che rende speciali eventi come il Torino Film Festival: momenti in cui l’arte si fa conversazione, dove le tecniche diventano aneddoti, e dove i grandi maestri si rivelano, alla fine, umani come tutti noi.

Per una volta Spike Lee possiamo dire e’ Spike Qui… a Torino.

Filosofia della Reinterpretazione vs Remake

Quote in inglese:

“Here’s the thing, very good question. I do not do remakes. In film school, one of the great things about film school, especially at NYU, was that I was introduced to world cinema. De Sica, Fellini, Kurosawa. And the premise for Rashomon, I used for She’s Gotta Have It. I mean, there’s even a term called Rashomon effect. Meaning, when you have 10 people, 10 people have a different opinion. And so I used that for Lola Dawn, where the main character, Lola Dawn, had three boyfriends at the same time. I got to meet the master filmmaker Kurosawa. And in my editing room is a beautiful portrait of him, and he signed it to me. He did his signatures in white ink with a paintbrush. And while we were editing the film, we placed the autographed portrait very strategically, so he was looking over my shoulder while we were editing the film. And I felt his presence. So anytime there was something my editor Barry, my longtime editor, Barry Brown, and I would have, we don’t know, I would turn my shoulder. What do you think, Kurosawa? What do you think? Mr. Kurosawa, what do you think? And he would let me know. Yea or nay. This is a reinterpretation. Not a remake. I’ll give an example. Many of you are cinephiles, so you’ve seen The Sound of Music, and you saw Julie Andrews singing My Favorite Things. Classic. Now, let me give you an example of reinterpretation. John Coltrane. Listen, look. No hate on Julie Andrews. There’s two great things. Rodgers and Hammerstein, Sound of Music, My Favorite Things. John Coltrane, a jazz interpretation of a great song. So that was my thing. On this film, I was not doing a remake. I was doing a jazz interpretation of Kurosawa’s High and Low. In fact we even changed, kind of twisted the title. It’s not High and Low, it’s Highest 2 Lowest. And it’s the number two.”

Traduzione italiana:

“Ecco la cosa, ottima domanda. Io non faccio remake. Alla scuola di cinema, una delle cose fantastiche della scuola di cinema, specialmente alla NYU, è stata che sono stato introdotto al cinema mondiale. De Sica, Fellini, Kurosawa. E la premessa di Rashomon, l’ho usata per She’s Gotta Have It. Voglio dire, c’è persino un termine chiamato effetto Rashomon. Nel senso che quando hai 10 persone, 10 persone hanno un’opinione diversa. E quindi l’ho usato per Lola Dawn, dove il personaggio principale, Lola Dawn, aveva tre fidanzati allo stesso tempo. Ho potuto incontrare il maestro regista Kurosawa. E nella mia sala di montaggio c’è un bellissimo ritratto di lui, e me l’ha firmato. Faceva le sue firme con inchiostro bianco con un pennello. E mentre montavamo il film, abbiamo posizionato il ritratto autografato molto strategicamente, così mi guardava alle spalle mentre montavamo il film. E sentivo la sua presenza. Quindi ogni volta che c’era qualcosa che io e il mio montatore Barry, il mio montatore di lunga data, Barry Brown, avevamo, non sapevamo, mi giravo sulla spalla. Che ne pensi, Kurosawa? Che ne pensi? Signor Kurosawa, che ne pensi? E lui mi faceva sapere. Sì o no. Questa è una reinterpretazione. Non un remake. Vi faccio un esempio. Molti di voi sono cinefili, quindi avete visto Tutti insieme appassionatamente, e avete visto Julie Andrews cantare My Favorite Things. Classico. Ora, lasciatemi darvi un esempio di reinterpretazione. John Coltrane. Ascoltate, guardate. Niente contro Julie Andrews. Ci sono due cose grandiose. Rodgers e Hammerstein, Tutti insieme appassionatamente, My Favorite Things. John Coltrane, un’interpretazione jazz di una grande canzone. Quindi quella era la mia cosa. Su questo film, non stavo facendo un remake. Stavo facendo un’interpretazione jazz di Cielo e Terra di Kurosawa. Infatti abbiamo anche cambiato, un po’ stravolto il titolo. Non è High and Low, è Highest 2 Lowest. Ed è il numero due.”

L’Incontro con il Papa

Quote in inglese:

“Here’s the thing. I knew nothing about what happened. I got an email from his office. I thought it was fake. Another word in English for fake is fugazi. Now, the Pope is from Chicago. And something that you might not know, the Pope, his people, Black. His people... Yes. Do your research. He comes, his family comes from New Orleans. Creole. A lot of intermarriage. So, the Black Pope’s been claimed. We call him our brother. So anyway, once I knew I was going to get an audience with the Pope, I thought automatically, the Pope went to college at Villanova. Villanova University. And the New York Knicks had three players from Villanova. And so, I said, ‘I got to do this, right? If I get the chance, I need to give a New York Knick jersey as a gift.’ So it’s 14 Pope. Number 14, and on the back it said, ‘Pope Leo.’ So when the Pope came out and sat down, I was in the first row. I took the jersey out of the bag and just held it up before we got to... He looked at me before his speech on cinema. And he laughed, he smiled. So that’s where the connection was made. And so when he smiled back at me holding up the jersey, I knew I was safe in presenting it. And I’m waiting to get it back. I autographed for him the one he held up. But I gave him another one to sign for me. Yes, I believe him. I mean, he was just... The Pope was telling the truth as he saw it as far as how cinema could be a great impact for good on this God’s Earth. So, people never knew who I was, know who I am now, because of this picture of the Pope holding up the jersey I gave to him as a gift. That went around the world.”

Rapporto Cinematografico con New York e Influenza Familiare

Quote in inglese:

“My relation with New York? Give me another question. I lived there. What kind of question is that? I was not born in New York. I was born in Atlanta, Georgia. And my family moved north when I was three years old. So I’m a New Yorker. I don’t remember not ever living in New York City except when I went back to Atlanta for college. I went to Morehouse College, Atlanta, Georgia. I mean, my mother was a cinephile and my father hated movies. So I’m the oldest of five and I was my mother’s movie date, and we went to movies. In fact, in this new film Highest 2 Lowest, I’m giving you a French word. That stuff on the subway in Highest 2 Lowest, that’s The French Connection. That’s Billy Friedkin. That, the chase, where the late great Gene Hackman starts as the character of Popeye the Dope, which is a real character, he was a real detective. That chase began at the subway stop by high school. So when I was in high school, I was in class when they filmed that. We didn’t know it ‘til the film came out a year later and we saw the sign. So, I was a cinephile even before I knew what the word was. I was my mother’s... My mother was a cinephile. And I was her movie date. So looking back, that’s where the seeds were planted for... I went to the movies going to the movies to have fun. I never thought... I didn’t know who the director was, what they did. I didn’t read the credits. I just went to the movies. So those seeds were planted very early. And that’s why I’ve come to really learn parents, what parents can do, and what they don’t do. And I’m just grateful my parents, my father agreed to jazz. My father did the scores. He did the score for my films. He did the score for She’s Gotta Have It, School Daze, Do the Right Thing, and both of them movies. So music was always played in the house. So I got introduced to film and music at an early age, not knowing somewhere down the line in my life they would come back and be my life.”
